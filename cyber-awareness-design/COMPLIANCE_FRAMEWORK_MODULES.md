# Compliance, IT Frameworks & Cybersecurity Concepts Module Add-on

## Purpose

Add a structured compliance and cyber maturity layer to the awareness platform. This makes the product useful not only for phishing awareness, but also for regulated industries, audit readiness, SOC teams, IT support teams, and cyber governance programmes.

## New Training Categories

### 1. RBI / BFSI Cyber Awareness Pack

Audience:
- Banks
- NBFCs
- Fintechs
- Payment companies
- Insurance and financial operations teams

Topics:
- Customer data protection
- Digital payment fraud
- UPI fraud and social engineering
- Account takeover awareness
- Vendor and outsourcing risk
- Secure use of email and file sharing
- Incident escalation
- Branch/user cyber hygiene
- Role-based finance fraud scenarios

Layman example:

A customer receives a fake UPI collect request after speaking to someone pretending to be bank support. The module teaches employees how social engineering works and why customer data must not leak through calls, emails, screenshots or WhatsApp.

### 2. SEBI / Securities Market Pack

Audience:
- Stock brokers
- AMCs
- Exchanges
- Depositories
- Investment advisors
- Portfolio managers
- Market intermediaries

Topics:
- Client data handling
- Trading account takeover awareness
- Fake investment portals
- Payment redirection
- Phishing against relationship managers
- Vendor risk
- Incident reporting and escalation
- Cyber hygiene for market-facing teams

Layman example:

A relationship manager receives a file named “Client Portfolio Update.xlsm” from an unknown sender. The module teaches macro risks, attachment caution and reporting steps.

### 5. NIST Cybersecurity Framework Awareness

Audience:
- Managers
- IT
- Risk
- Compliance
- Security teams

Simple explanation:

NIST CSF can be explained as five business habits:

1. Identify: Know what you have.
2. Protect: Keep it safe.
3. Detect: Notice when something is wrong.
4. Respond: Act quickly.
5. Recover: Restore normal operations.

Module ideas:
- NIST for non-technical managers
- NIST for IT teams
- Mapping awareness modules to NIST functions

### 9. MITRE ATT&CK Awareness

Audience:
- Intermediate and expert users
- SOC
- IT
- Security teams
- Management awareness version

Simple explanation:

MITRE ATT&CK is a map of attacker behaviour. It helps defenders understand what attackers commonly try, how to detect it and how to improve controls.

Safe module topics:
- What are tactics and techniques?
- Initial access examples such as phishing
- Credential misuse awareness
- Lateral movement concept
- Detection and response mapping
- How training reduces initial access risk

Safety note:
This module should remain defensive. It should not teach exploitation, evasion, malware deployment or credential theft.

### 10. Red Team / Blue Team / Purple Team Concepts

Audience:
- All users for concept module
- Expert version for IT/security

Simple explanation:
- Red Team: authorised team that tests defences.
- Blue Team: defenders who monitor, detect and respond.
- Purple Team: red and blue working together to improve security.

Modules:
- Red Team vs real attacker
- Blue Team and SOC basics
- Purple Team improvement cycle
- Lessons from simulation
- How phishing simulation supports purple teaming

### 12. MTTD / MTTR Metrics

The user mentioned “mist, mmtr”; this is likely MTTD/MTTR or NIST/MITRE. Include both.

MTTD: Mean Time To Detect
MTTR: Mean Time To Respond/Recover

Layman explanation:
- MTTD: How long it takes to notice something is wrong.
- MTTR: How long it takes to fix or recover.

Platform reporting can show:
- Average phishing report time
- Time from click to report
- Time from campaign launch to detection
- Time from contribution submission to approval
- Training completion delay
